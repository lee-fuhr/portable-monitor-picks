Frequently asked questions

Do portable monitors need their own power supply?

Most modern portable monitors can be powered entirely over a single USB-C cable from your laptop, which is one of their main advantages. Some models require a specific wattage from that cable, and older or lower-end laptops may not provide enough power from their USB-C ports. A few portable monitors include a separate power adapter as a fallback, but the goal is usually one cable for both video and power. Always check the minimum power delivery spec against what your laptop's USB-C port actually outputs.

What size portable monitor is most practical for travel?

Fifteen to sixteen inches is the sweet spot for most people. It's large enough to be genuinely useful as a second screen, and a 15-inch panel in a slim housing fits in most laptop bags alongside a 14 or 15-inch laptop. Thirteen-inch models are lighter and more packable but feel cramped for real multitasking work. Anything 17 inches or above starts to compete with the laptop in weight and bag space.

Is 1080p or 1440p better for a portable monitor?

At 15 to 16 inches, 1440p delivers noticeably sharper text and more desktop room than 1080p, and most modern laptops can drive it easily. 1080p is still perfectly usable for general work and cuts the price down. For anything involving precise visual work, 1440p is worth the premium. 4K at this screen size is technically impressive but harder to drive from a laptop and often requires scaling that erases the real-estate advantage.

What connection does a portable monitor use?

Most good portable monitors connect over USB-C, which carries both the video signal and power delivery in a single cable. Some include a mini-HDMI port as a fallback for computers without USB-C video output. DisplayPort Alt Mode over USB-C is the gold standard for compatibility. Thunderbolt 4 ports on MacBooks and modern Intel laptops handle USB-C portable monitors reliably, but check your laptop's USB-C port spec to confirm it supports video output, since charging-only ports exist on some machines.

Can I use a portable monitor with an iPad or phone?

Many portable monitors support USB-C video input from an iPad Pro or Android phone with DisplayPort support. iPhone support requires a USB-C Lightning or USB-C cable combined with Stage Manager on iPadOS, or a specific dock. Compatibility is narrower than with laptops, so check the monitor manufacturer's listed device compatibility rather than assuming it will work from any USB-C device.
