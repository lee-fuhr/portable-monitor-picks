Best portable monitor for a laptop

Quick answer

For most people, the ASUS ZenScreen MB16QHG is the portable monitor to buy, it strikes the best balance of sharpness, brightness, and build quality.

If you use a MacBook, the LG gram +view 16MQ70 integrates seamlessly; on a tight budget the ViewSonic VA1655 gets the job done; and the INNOCN 15B1F offers extra features for not much more.

Laptops are getting thinner, but their screens aren’t getting bigger. A portable monitor turns a cramped 13- or 14-inch display into a true dual-screen workstation, whether you’re editing spreadsheets in a coffee shop, reviewing code in a hotel room, or just want a second screen for your console. The trick is finding one that’s light enough to carry, bright enough to see outdoors, and easy to connect without a tangle of adapters. The key specs to weigh are panel type, resolution, weight, and connectivity. IPS screens offer the best all-around color and viewing angle; a 1080p panel is fine for most work, but a 1440p or 4K screen makes text and images noticeably crisper. Look for USB-C with DisplayPort Alt Mode, that’s what lets you run video and power through a single cable. Battery life (if it has one) matters, but competitive options skip internal batteries to save weight.

Frequently asked questions

Which laptops work best with portable monitors?

Laptops with USB-C ports that support DisplayPort Alt Mode and Power Delivery work best, since they can drive the monitor and sometimes power it from a single cable. This includes most MacBooks, Dell XPS, Lenovo ThinkPads, and recent HP Spectres. Chromebooks with USB-C also work well. Older laptops with only USB-A ports or a single HDMI output need a separate hub or adapter and may need an external power source for the monitor.

Does a portable monitor slow down my laptop?

Running a second display puts a small additional load on your GPU to render the extra pixels, which can modestly reduce battery life. The impact is usually minor on modern laptops with dedicated GPUs, and on recent integrated-GPU laptops it's also manageable. The bigger battery draw comes from the monitor itself drawing power from the laptop's USB-C port. Expect to lose roughly 20 to 30 percent of battery runtime compared to using just the laptop screen, though this varies widely by monitor brightness and laptop hardware.

Do I need special software to use a portable monitor with a laptop?

No, in most cases. A portable monitor plugged in over USB-C or HDMI appears to the operating system as a standard external display, and the laptop detects it automatically. Windows, macOS, and most Linux distributions handle portable monitors without any drivers or special software. Some monitors include companion software for brightness and input switching, but it's optional, not required for basic operation.
